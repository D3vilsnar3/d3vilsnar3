Launch Monitor ML Suite

A third-party launch-monitor portal measures ball-flight data during a shot, but the hardest metrics to measure directly — spin rate and spin axis — are also the most valuable to predict from signals that are cheap to capture. A Chrome extension scraped 1,218 bulk shot exports from that portal to build a training set, since the only way to know what's predictable from the easy metrics is to have both at scale.

Three neural networks were trained on the resulting dataset — one each for club speed, spin rate, and spin axis — then converted to CoreML and shipped for on-device iOS inference. Club speed error dropped from a 20.5 km/h legacy estimate to 1.99 km/h. Spin rate error dropped from roughly 3,978 rpm to 597 rpm on held-out test data; spin axis classification reached 72% test accuracy. The model also exposed a real gap worth being honest about: accuracy on the internal test set doesn't fully transfer to real sensor ground truth yet, which is the next calibration problem rather than a solved one.

  • 1,218 bulk exports scraped via a purpose-built Chrome extension
  • Three Dense neural nets — club speed, spin rate, spin axis — trained on millions of rows
  • Club speed error: 20.5 km/h (legacy) -> 1.99 km/h
  • Spin rate error: ~3,978 rpm (legacy) -> 597 rpm
  • Converted to CoreML and shipped for on-device iOS inference
